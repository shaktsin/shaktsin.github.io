FailFast-Fargate: Predictive Container Restart Policies for SLO-Driven ECS/Fargate Services

Shakti Pratap Singh

Independent Researcher · Preprint · Version v1 · Published June 13, 2026

Read the full paper (PDF, 214 kB) · Zenodo / DOI

Abstract

Tasks in ECS/Fargate do not undergo replacements until they have either failed or failed health checks. Nonetheless, many cases of production issues can be attributed to task degradations, which include memory growth, retry storms, and timeouts in upstreams. Degradations take long periods before affecting tasks’ health or the SLO error budgets. In this paper, we introduce FailFast-Fargate, a proactive task-replacement approach for SLO-based ECS/Fargate applications. FailFast-Fargate evaluates the risk of SLO violations by each task through task telemetry and trace data within a short horizon. FailFast-Fargate then makes decisions on task replacements based on the cost-benefit ratio where the estimated cost of leaving a failing task is compared against its estimated cost of replacing it, and the task with higher cost is replaced. Since FailFast-Fargate does not involve any modifications of the AWS ECS architecture, it makes use of standard ECS mechanisms while respecting capacity and rollout constraints. Our experiments involving injection of synthetic task degradations reveal that FailFast-Fargate mitigates SLO violations by decreasing error-budget burn time and duration while maintaining a steady stream of task restarts and preventing restart storms.

Keywords: ECS, Fargate, SLO, error budget, predictive restart, survival analysis, self-healing
